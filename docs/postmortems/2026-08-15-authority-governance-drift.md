# Authority Governance Drift Postmortem

## Purpose and boundary

This is a blameless account of how unsupported or insufficiently reviewed
conclusions repeatedly reached requirements, implementation, tests, and visible
behavior. It records causes, affected artifact families, and preventive work.
It does not decide product meaning, validate a current implementation, or
replace the five permanent authorities under `docs/`.

The clean commit `3b2456a` is the preserved unverified baseline. “Clean” means
only that the Git worktree had no uncommitted changes. It does not make that
revision, its runtime roster, or its completed milestones semantically trusted.

## Impact

The incident reduced confidence that later verticals consistently applied
settled product rules. Current artifacts must be re-audited instead of being
treated as accepted templates. The observed failure families include:

- shield and other survival value entering damage-oriented setup or Result
  reasoning after that admission boundary had already been challenged;
- enemy-recipient wording being transformed into a generic Agent-provided
  `DMG Taken` modifier category without an independently established consumer;
- Proto Punk being re-admitted from trigger legality or shield utility without
  completing the competitive whole-package and same-axis comparison;
- W-Engine selectors gaining holder-local `Inactive` presentation and retaining
  source-like effect prose without an accepted selector-semantic change; and
- portrait framing regressing for Koleda, Pulchra, Nekomata, Ben, and Zhao after
  requested visual safeguards were deferred.

These bullets identify audit targets and visible symptoms. They are not the
corrected product answers; those must come from permanent owners, accepted
Authority Change Records when needed, and later bounded corrections.

## What happened

1. The repository recorded permanent rules and a workflow learning warning
   that secondary requirements cannot validate themselves.
2. Later bounded work sometimes inferred a local conclusion, wrote that
   conclusion into a supporting requirement, implemented it, and generated
   tests from the same premise.
3. Reviews and passing checks then proved internal agreement with the new
   premise rather than independent support from the permanent owner and a
   contrasting current consumer.
4. Re-grounding reports often began from recent requirements and implementation
   outcomes. Even when operationally strong, they did not consistently reopen
   previously embedded modifier, candidate, selector, or visual assumptions.
5. The roadmap authorized automatic continuation when no semantic gate was
   noticed, while CONTRIBUTING still said CI and visual regression lacked a
   current consumer. More verticals therefore accumulated before the repeated
   pattern became a repository-wide recovery event.

## Root causes

### Secondary evidence formed a closed validation loop

Requirements, implementation, tests, and review were sometimes derived from
one controller conclusion. No enforced transaction required the conclusion to
survive permanent-rule, nearest-consumer, and active-contrast review first.

### Authority changes and dependent work were not separated

A new interpretation could enter a requirement or permanent document in the
same flow as the code it justified. Later readers saw several mutually
supporting artifacts but not an independently approved authority transition.

### Tests were allowed to imply semantic support

Automated tests accurately checked encoded behavior, but their success was
occasionally reported as evidence that the premise itself was correct. The
test suite had no machine-enforced distinction between implementation fidelity
and product-authority support.

### Review ownership and evidence were advisory

There was no protected remote branch, required policy status, CODEOWNER gate,
or durable fresh-review evidence. A controller could acknowledge an earlier
warning and still merge a later change that repeated it.

### Visual verification remained a per-task promise

Original-asset inspection and the four responsive portrait destinations were
documented, but there was no stable approved baseline or remote regression
gate. Deferring the requested safeguard work left the same calibration error
class available to recur.

## Contributing conditions

- Long-running controller tasks and frequent context compaction made recent
  conclusions easier to retrieve than the exact owning rule and contrast.
- Continuous authorization to proceed through settled verticals increased the
  cost of a missed stop signal.
- Shared files and mechanism tests did not identify which high-risk rule a
  changed conclusion relied on.
- The roadmap's continuation language and CONTRIBUTING's obsolete process
  triggers understated the now-demonstrated need for remote and visual gates.
- A requested earlier ordering of visual safeguards was not adopted; the
  decision to defer that work contributed directly to repeated portrait drift.

None of these conditions transfers responsibility to the product owner. The
controller was responsible for stopping when authority support or acceptance
evidence was missing.

## Detection gaps

The incident was not reliably detected by clean Git status, completed-plan
milestones, passing tests, production builds, browser checks, or multiple
reviewers. Those signals checked consistency after the premise had already
entered the work. Detection occurred when current behavior was compared again
with prior settled boundaries and superficially similar but contrasting
consumers.
